## User Interview: Mr. Ramachandrappa M. ("Ramu Anna")
- Three Direct Quotes:
  1. "Students come running at 1:45 PM panting 'Anna! External examiner viva at 2:30 PM, please give this shirt!' If I light the coal for just their one shirt, I burn 65 rupees of coal to earn 8 rupees. My afternoon profit becomes zero."
  2. "I don't need a computer to tell me how to iron a pant. I need to know: if I light this stove right now, will I get at least 15 clothes before 4 o'clock, or will I sit alone swatting flies while my coal turns into white ash?"
  3. "The charcoal from Yeshwanthpur mandi went from Rs. 42 to Rs. 50 a kilo this monsoon. One sack lasts me four days only. Coal is like ice - once you light it, it melts away whether you touch clothes or not."

## Surprising Insights:
1. Mechanical Weight vs. Human Fatigue: A domestic electric iron weighs 1.2 kg. To flatten stiff starched drill cotton lab aprons, a person has to push down with their whole upper-body weight. The brass charcoal iron weighs 6.5 kg empty and 7.5 kg with coal; gravity provides the compression force for free.
2. Grid Limits: Turning on an electric iron trips the kiosk's shared 10A breaker when the tea stall runs its grinder.
3. Thermal Hierarchy: Synthetics melt if pressed on freshly lit coal (220C). You MUST press heavy cottons first, and synthetics last as coals cool.
